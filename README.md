# Limitations of Generative AI in Content Creation: A Review

A published review paper on what generative AI gets wrong when it is used to make content.

## Published in

International Journal of Scientific Research & Engineering Trends (IJSRET), Volume 12, Issue 5, Sep-Oct 2026. ISSN (Online): 2395-566X.

## Authors

- Mayur Rajaram Chavan
- Prof. Abhishek Popat Bangar
- Prof. S. B. Bhosale
- Prof. Karad V. A.

Department of Computer Engineering, Jaihind College of Engineering, Kuran, Maharashtra, India.

## What the paper does

Generative AI tools can draft stories, news and social media posts in seconds, but smooth output says nothing about whether it is correct, original, fair or safe to use. This paper reviews five studies that look at the limits from different angles:

- How creators try to use generative AI responsibly (Kim et al., 2026)
- Whether AI help makes stories better but more alike (Doshi & Hauser, 2024)
- What prompting can and cannot do in creative practice (Casacuberta & Guersenzvaig, 2025)
- Copyright and ownership questions (Al-Busaidi et al., 2024)
- Gender and racial bias in AI-written news (Fang et al., 2024)

## What came out of it

- AI help raised creativity scores for individual writers, most for those who were less creative to begin with — but the AI-assisted stories were more similar to each other, so the group as a whole became less original.
- Creators deal with the risks in everyday work: fact-checking, moderating, saying when AI was used, and protecting private information.
- Prompting cannot fully capture the tacit knowledge behind creative work, so it can limit creative control.
- Ownership of generated material is still unsettled, and rules differ between countries.
- AI-written news showed notable gender and racial bias; fluent writing should never be taken as neutral writing.

This is a focused narrative review of five selected papers, not a systematic review.
